About

Sam Rudd

Painter, working in oils in the north of England.

I paint the ordinary parts of the landscape — a verge, a field margin, the turn of a river — where the light does something worth staying for.

Most of the work starts outdoors, in small oil studies made quickly and in whatever weather is going. Those studies are rarely finished pictures; they are notes about colour and about how the light was behaving on a particular morning. The larger paintings are built afterwards in the studio, where colour can be pushed a little further than the day allowed, and where a painting can be left alone for a few weeks and then looked at again honestly.

I work in series, usually tied to a place I keep going back to. The dales paintings have run for several years now; the coastal and estuary work came later, out of a long stretch of drawing on the Lancashire sands. Subjects tend to repeat because the light never does.

Materials

Oil on canvas, linen or gesso-primed board. Pieces are painted on properly prepared supports and finished with a removable varnish, so they can be cleaned and cared for in the long term. Larger works are stretched on hardwood bars; smaller boards are usually framed in an unfinished oak tray frame.
